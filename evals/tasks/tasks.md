# Held-out task set: Mapbox GL JS v3.35.2

Ten requests of the kind web developers give a coding agent when building with Mapbox GL JS. Each asks for one page, `index.html`, that loads GL JS v3.35.2 from Mapbox's CDN, takes its token from `window.MAPBOX_ACCESS_TOKEN`, fills an 800 × 600 px window and assigns its map to `window.map` (the full prompt is in `evals/README.md`). The agent sees a task's text up to its `Check:` sentence, and any file the task names is in its folder.

Each page is opened once in headless Chromium, and its check runs after the map first goes idle. Checks read the map through `window.map`, and use pixels only to tell colours apart coarsely. Screen positions count from the top-left corner. In the prose, coordinates are given as latitude and longitude, the way people usually write them. Pages must convert them to GL JS's `[longitude, latitude]` order.

**1. Shop markers with popups (markers and popups).** A bakery has three shops in Lisbon, given here as (latitude, longitude): Alfama (38.7114, -9.1300), Bairro Alto (38.7134, -9.1458) and Belém (38.6976, -9.2063). On the Mapbox Standard style, put Mapbox's default marker, coloured #d62828, on each shop, and make clicking a marker open a popup showing that shop's name exactly as written here. The map must open framed on the three shops with 80 px of padding on every side. Check: there are exactly three markers; each shop's position projects inside (±6 px) the box of a marker element that contains something coloured rgb(214, 40, 40); all three positions are in the viewport; the zoom is within 0.35 of the zoom `cameraForBounds` gives for the three shops with 80 px padding; and clicking each marker opens a popup whose text contains that shop's name.

**2. Population density choropleth (data-driven styling).** `districts.geojson` holds six district polygons in Madrid, each with a numeric `density` property. On the Mapbox Standard style, centred on 40.42° N, 3.70° W at zoom 11.5, draw them as a fill layer with the id `density`, at fill opacity 0.75, coloured by density in four classes: below 10 is #edf8fb; 10 or more but below 50 is #b3cde3; 50 or more but below 100 is #8c96c6; 100 or more is #88419d. The districts must sit beneath the basemap's roads and labels. Check: layer `density` is a fill layer in the Standard style's `bottom` slot, and at each district's centre the rendered feature's evaluated fill colour equals its class colour (±2 per channel) and its fill opacity is 0.75 (±0.01); the six densities are 4, 10, 32, 50, 87 and 240, so two of them sit exactly on a class boundary.

**3. Clustered sightings (clustering).** `sightings.geojson` holds 120 bird sightings around Amsterdam, as points. Show them on Mapbox Dark (`mapbox://styles/mapbox/dark-v11`), centred on 52.37° N, 4.90° E at zoom 10, clustered with a cluster radius of 50 px and no clustering above zoom 14. Draw each cluster as a circle with its number of sightings written on it, and each sighting that is not in a cluster as a small circle. Clicking a cluster must move the map so that it is centred on that cluster at the cluster's expansion zoom, the zoom at which it breaks apart. Check: a GeoJSON source has clustering on, with radius 50 and `clusterMaxZoom` 14; the clusters and single points drawn account for all 120 sightings, in at least two clusters, each labelled with its point count; and after the largest cluster is clicked and the camera stops, the zoom is within 0.25 of that cluster's expansion zoom (read from the source) and the cluster is within 40 px of the map's centre.

**4. Highlight on hover (feature-state interaction).** `zones.geojson` holds three adjacent delivery zones in Toronto, named West, Centre and East in the property `zone`. On Mapbox Streets (`mapbox://styles/mapbox/streets-v12`), centred on 43.65° N, 79.39° W at zoom 13, fill them #8ecae6 at full opacity. While the mouse pointer is over a zone, that zone alone must be filled #e63946; when the pointer moves to another zone, or off all of them, the colour must go back to #8ecae6. Check: a 15 × 15 px patch at each zone's centre is called red or blue by counting its pixels within 60 RGB units of #e63946 and of #8ecae6, and takes the colour with more such pixels if they make up at least 30 % of the patch; all three are blue before any hover; with the pointer moved over West, then Centre, then East, only the zone under the pointer is red; and with the pointer moved off the zones, all three are blue again.

**5. Bike docks with a custom icon (symbols and icons).** `docks.geojson` holds 12 bike-share docks in central Copenhagen, each with a `name`, and `dock.png` is the 32 × 32 px icon for them. On Mapbox Outdoors (`mapbox://styles/mapbox/outdoors-v12`), centred on 55.6800° N, 12.5700° E at zoom 13, show each dock with that icon at its natural size, centred on the dock's position, and the dock's name as a 12 px label below the icon. At this zoom the docks are close together. All 12 icons must be shown, even where icons overlap each other or a label; a label may be left out where it would collide with another. Check: a rendered-feature query over the viewport returns all 12 docks from symbol layers whose icon image is loaded and drawn at icon size 1, and the icon's magenta covers at least 40 % of a 5 × 5 px patch at the positions of at least 9 of the 12 docks.

**6. Georeferenced image overlay (image sources).** `plan.png` is a 320 × 240 px plan of part of Prague. On Mapbox Light (`mapbox://styles/mapbox/light-v11`), centred on 50.085° N, 14.42° E at zoom 13, lay it on the map at 80 % opacity with its top-left corner at 50.095° N, 14.40° E, its top-right corner at 50.095° N, 14.44° E, its bottom-right corner at 50.075° N, 14.44° E and its bottom-left corner at 50.075° N, 14.40° E. Check: a raster layer draws an image source at raster opacity 0.8 (±0.01), and the mean colour of a 9 × 9 px patch at the centre of each quarter of that area is nearest to the colour of the same quarter of the image, which is red at the top left, green at the top right, blue at the bottom right and yellow at the bottom left.

**7. Tokyo at night (basemap configuration).** Show central Tokyo on the Mapbox Standard style, centred on 35.6812° N, 139.7671° E at zoom 15 with a pitch of 60°, with the basemap set to the night light preset and the monochrome theme, and with its point-of-interest labels hidden. Check: the basemap's `lightPreset` is `night`, its `theme` is `monochrome` and its `showPointOfInterestLabels` is false; a query of the basemap's `poi` featureset finds no rendered point of interest other than transit stops (which `showTransitLabels` governs); the camera is as asked (within 30 m, 0.05 zoom levels and 0.5°); and over 100 patches on a 10 × 10 grid of the screenshot the mean brightness is below 90 of 255 while the brightest and darkest patches differ by more than 20, so the map is dark but drawn.

**8. Crater Lake in 3D (terrain).** Show Crater Lake, Oregon, in 3D on Mapbox Satellite Streets (`mapbox://styles/mapbox/satellite-streets-v12`), with terrain from the Mapbox Terrain-DEM v1 tileset (`mapbox://mapbox.mapbox-terrain-dem-v1`) exaggerated 1.5 times, and the camera centred on 42.9440° N, 122.0980° W at zoom 12, pitch 60° and bearing 0. Once the terrain has loaded, read from it the height above sea level of the ground at that centre point, which is the lake's surface, round it to a whole number of metres, and show it in an element with id `elevation`, written like `1234 m`. Check: terrain is on, from a raster-dem source, with exaggeration 1.5; the camera is as asked (within 50 m, 0.1 zoom levels and 1°); the terrain's own unexaggerated elevation at the centre point is between 1860 and 1905 m (the lake's surface is at 1883 m); and the number shown in `#elevation` is within 15 m of it.

**9. Driving route (directions).** On Mapbox Navigation Day (`mapbox://styles/mapbox/navigation-day-v1`), get the driving route from the Brandenburg Gate (52.5163° N, 13.3777° E) to Alexanderplatz (52.5219° N, 13.4132° E) from the Mapbox Directions API, draw it as a #3a86ff line 5 px wide, fit the map so the whole route is in view, and show the route's length in kilometres, to one decimal place, in an element with id `distance`, written like `12.3 km`. Check: a line layer draws a GeoJSON line that starts within 300 m of the Brandenburg Gate and ends within 300 m of Alexanderplatz, is 2 to 8 km long, has at least 95 % of its points in the viewport and is drawn in #3a86ff at width 5; and `#distance` shows a number within 15 % of the drawn line's length.

**10. Address search (geocoding).** Make a small address search over the Mapbox Standard style. The map opens on Great Britain, centred on 54.5° N, 3.0° W at zoom 5. In the top-left corner, above the map, put a text input with id `search` and a button with id `go`. When the button is clicked, look up the typed text with the Mapbox Geocoding API, then move the map to the first result at zoom 16 and put a marker there, replacing any marker from an earlier search. Check: after `10 Downing Street, London` is typed into `#search` and `#go` is clicked, at least one Geocoding API request is made, and once the camera stops the map is centred within 300 m of 51.5034° N, 0.1277° W at zoom 16 (±0.1), with exactly one marker, whose box contains the map's centre (±8 px).
