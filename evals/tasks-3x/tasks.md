# tasks-3x: ten held-out Mapbox GL JS v3.35.2 tasks

Each task below is one paragraph, `**N. Title (area).** text… Check: …`. An agent sees the paragraph up to `Check:`;
the check itself is `checks.mjs`. The files a task names are in `data/taskN/` (written by `make_data.mjs`). Each task
mainly exercises one GL JS 3.x feature:

| task | feature it exercises |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3D `model` layer on a `model` source, glTF placed and oriented |
| 2 | `addModel` and a `model` layer instanced on GeoJSON points (rotation and tint per feature) |
| 3 | the `clip` layer over Mapbox Standard |
| 4 | the `raster-particle` layer on a `raster-array` source (mapbox.gfs-winds) |
| 5 | the procedural `building` layer type |
| 6 | style imports: config on the Standard import, a layer placed in a slot |
| 7 | style imports at runtime: `updateImport` on the basemap import |
| 8 | Interactions API: a featureset target and feature state on Standard's `buildings` |
| 9 | Interactions API: a featureset target on Standard's `poi` |
| 10 | Interactions API on a root layer, feature state on GeoJSON without ids |

The area in each title is written as a developer would name it, since the agent sees it.

**1. Pavilion on the Great Lawn (3D model).** We're proposing a temporary pavilion for the Great Lawn in Central Park, and we have it as a glTF file, pavilion.glb. It is authored in metres, and its long side runs along the model's x axis. On Mapbox's Standard style, show it standing at longitude −73.9662, latitude 40.7815 at its real size, turned 90° so that its long side runs north–south. Open the map on it at zoom 17, pitched to 60°. Check: the style has a model layer; from a top-down view at zoom 18.5 (Standard's labels and trees hidden), the pavilion's red roof covers the given point and at least 3 of 4 points 12–16 m north and south of it, at most 1 of 4 points 12–16 m east and west of it, and neither point 30 m north or south (a 40 m × 12 m footprint turned north–south, at real size).

**2. Delivery vans at the depot (3D models).** Our test fleet is parked on Tempelhofer Feld in Berlin. vans.geojson has one point per van, with its `heading` in degrees clockwise from north and its `status`, either "late" or "on-time". On Mapbox's Standard style, show every van as our 3D model van.glb (to scale, in metres; in the file the van faces north), each turned to its heading and coloured by its status: late vans red (#e53935), on-time vans green (#43a047). Centre the map on longitude 13.403, latitude 52.474 at zoom 18. Check: a model layer draws vans.geojson; GL JS's own evaluation of its rotation for each van is [0, 0, heading] (±2°); and from a top-down view at zoom 18 (Standard's labels and trees hidden), at least 7 of the 8 vans are drawn red if late and green if on-time, sampled at each van's roof.

**3. Clearing the redevelopment site (basemap 3D removal).** We're presenting a redevelopment of the block around Square du Temple in Paris; site.geojson is the site boundary. On Mapbox's Standard style, remove the existing 3D buildings and the 3D trees inside the site so that the empty plot shows, and leave everything outside the site as it is. Open the map at longitude 2.3606, latitude 48.8647, zoom 17, pitched 55°. Check: one clip layer covers five sample points inside the site and none of four points 40% of the site's size beyond its edges, is active at the current zoom, comes after Standard's `3d-building` and `trees` layers in GL JS's render order (before them it removes nothing), lists `model` in `clip-layer-types` (without it Standard's instanced trees stay), and is not scoped away from the basemap.

**4. Global wind animation (wind particles).** On Mapbox's Dark style (mapbox://styles/mapbox/dark-v11), animate the 10 m winds from Mapbox's GFS winds tileset, mapbox.gfs-winds (its data layer is 10winds; speeds are in m/s), as moving particles over the whole world at zoom 1. Colour each particle by its wind speed: #3288bd (blue) at 0 m/s, #fee08b (yellow) at 10 m/s, and #d53e4f (red) at 20 m/s or faster, blending smoothly in between. Check: a raster-particle layer reads the 10winds layer of the mapbox.gfs-winds raster-array tileset, and in that layer's own colour ramp (256 colours spread over 0 to raster-particle-max-speed, which is what particles are drawn with) particles at 0, 10, 20 and 30 m/s get #3288bd, #fee08b, #d53e4f and #d53e4f (±30 per channel), at least half opaque.

**5. Planned buildings with real roofs (3D buildings).** footprints.geojson holds five planned buildings in Victoria Park, London, each with `height_m` and `roof` (gabled, hipped, flat, pyramidal or mansard). On Mapbox's Standard style, show them in 3D at their heights, each with the roof shape its `roof` property names, with terracotta roofs (#b5523b) and cream walls (#efe6d2). Centre the map on longitude −0.04, latitude 51.5365 at zoom 17, pitched 60°. Check: a procedural building layer draws footprints.geojson, and GL JS's own evaluation of it for every footprint gives building-height = height_m (±0.5 m) and building-roof-shape = roof, and building-color evaluated for the roof part is #b5523b and for the wall part #efe6d2 (±12 per channel; GL JS evaluates the colour once per part, with the part in the feature property `building-part`); from a top-down view at zoom 16.5 (Standard's labels and trees hidden) all five are queryable and at least four roofs are drawn terracotta at the footprint's centre (R at least 50 above G and 60 above B).

**6. Flood zones under the streets (basemap configuration).** Using Mapbox's Standard style with its dusk lighting and with the point-of-interest labels turned off, draw the flood zones in floodzones.geojson (HafenCity, Hamburg) as a blue fill (#1e88e5) at 50% opacity that sits beneath the roads and all the labels, so the streets stay readable on top of it. Centre the map on longitude 9.997, latitude 53.5418 at zoom 15. Check: the basemap import is Mapbox Standard with `lightPreset` "dusk" and `showPointOfInterestLabels` false, and a fill layer over floodzones.geojson is in Standard's `bottom` slot (the only slot beneath the roads), coloured #1e88e5 (±12 per channel) at an effective opacity of 0.5 (±0.06).

**7. Satellite toggle that keeps the route (basemap switching).** On Mapbox's Standard style, show the walking route in route.geojson (downtown San Francisco) as a 5 px line in #d81b60, centred on longitude −122.407, latitude 37.786 at zoom 14.5. Add a button labelled "Satellite" that switches the basemap to Mapbox Standard Satellite. After the switch the route must still be on the map, and the view must not move. Check: before the click the basemap is Mapbox Standard and the route is drawn as a 5 px (±0.5) #d81b60 (±12) line; after clicking the button and waiting for the new basemap, an import is Mapbox Standard Satellite, the route line layer is still in the style and drawn, and the centre has moved less than 30 m and the zoom less than 0.05.

**8. Pick a building (basemap buildings).** On Mapbox's Standard style, centred on longitude 2.3577, latitude 48.8612 (the Marais, Paris) at zoom 17, let the user click any building to highlight it in orange (#ff8c00). Clicking another building moves the highlight to that one, so only one building is highlighted at a time. Check: two buildings A (nearest the centre) and B (farthest from A) are found by querying Standard's `buildings` featureset; A is clicked, then B. Standard's labels are hidden first, so an orange POI icon cannot sit on a sample point. A building counts as highlighted if its feature state has `select` (or `highlight`) set and Standard's matching colour config is #ff8c00 (±20), or its pixel at the click point has turned orange since the start. After the first click A is highlighted; after the second B is highlighted and A is not.

**9. What's this place? (basemap points of interest).** On Mapbox's Standard style, centred on longitude −73.9897, latitude 40.7411 (Flatiron, New York) at zoom 16.5, clicking any of the map's own points of interest (shops, cafés, museums and so on) should open a popup showing that place's name. Check: named points of interest in view are found by querying Standard's `poi` featureset; the nearest to the centre is clicked at its point (up to three are tried, any popups closed between), and a `.mapboxgl-popup` must appear whose text contains the name of the POI under that point.

**10. Toggle districts (clickable data).** On Mapbox's Light style (mapbox://styles/mapbox/light-v11), show the six districts in districts.geojson (Austin, Texas) as grey fills (#9e9e9e at 60% opacity), centred on longitude −97.7431, latitude 30.2672 at zoom 13.6. Clicking a district selects it and turns it red (#d7301f); clicking a selected district turns it grey again. Several districts can be selected at once. Check: a fill layer draws districts.geojson; from a top-down view at zoom 13.6, districts D2 and D6 are read from 9 pixel samples each (red when at least 5 have R − G ≥ 50, grey when at most 2 do) at the start (grey/grey), after clicking D2 (red/grey), after clicking D6 (red/red), and after clicking D2 again (grey/red).

## Order of work

`make_data.mjs` writes `data/`; `reference/taskN.html` is a correct page and `reference/wrongN.html` the same page with
one mistake; `validation.md` records both pages through `node evals/run_eval.mjs --set tasks-3x --arms reference,wrong`.
